# Activity log

Not every workout follows a plan. You played pickleball, you walked the dog, you went to a yoga class. The Activity log keeps a short record of each, with no plan and no session. It is off until you turn it on in Settings.

## One entry, three choices

You open the Activity log for the logging date and tap to add. A sheet asks three things:

1. **Type.** Run, Walk, Bike, Swim, Hike, Pickleball, Tennis, Basketball, Yoga, Stretching, Cardio, or Other. With Other, you type a name.
2. **Duration.** It starts at 30 minutes. Plus and minus move it by 5, and you can hold them. It stays between 1 and 300 minutes.
3. **Intensity.** Easy, Moderate, or Hard.

You save, and the entry shows in the list for that date. The next time that you add an entry, the sheet starts on the type that you used last.

## Edit and delete

You tap an entry to change it. Delete asks for a second tap, "Tap again to confirm delete", so a slip of the thumb does not remove it. To fix a past day, you pick the date in the header, as the [logging date](logging-date.md) page tells.

## Why an activity is not a plan

A run has no sets, no routine, and no next step. If it were a plan, you would have to make a plan of one routine to log one run. So the app keeps two kinds apart: a structured practice that guides you, and a quick record of a thing that you did. A new sport is one more entry in the list of types — a line of configuration, not a new design. Pickleball came in that way, and it cost nothing.

## Where activities show

The Activity log card on the Overview lists the entries of the date. The week strip shows a mark on each day with an activity. Insights has an Activity Mix chart, which breaks down the activities that you logged in the selected range.

### Example

On Saturday you play pickleball for an hour. You open the Activity log, tap to add, select Pickleball, press plus six times to reach 60 minutes, select Moderate, and save. The Overview card for Saturday now lists the pickleball entry.
